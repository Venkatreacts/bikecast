## 5. Methodological findings (disclosed, not hidden)
1. **Polynomial split.** The earlier work used a random split. A chronological 70/30 split is dominated by the 2011→2012 growth (held-out R² ≈ −0.9, RMSE ≈ 2,260–2,300 for every degree), so it cannot demonstrate overfitting for a temperature-only function. Decision: keep the random split for this function-fitting illustration, report the chronological result as a sensitivity check, and use chronological splitting for every Unit 2 forecast.
2. **Degree 3 is not a sharp optimum.** Over 200 random splits M=3 wins 80 times and M=9 wins 82 times; the median held-out RMSE is lowest at M=3.
3. **Ridge** has almost no effect here (n = 13,903, p = 77); Huber is slightly worse; Bayesian regression's posterior mean equals a ridge solution (λ = α/β, verified), so it adds uncertainty, not accuracy.
4. **Raw-count Bayesian intervals** cover only 51.7% at peak hours (constant noise variance); log-scale intervals cover 93.1% / 94.1%.
5. **Probit approximation** is rough in the extreme tails (Monte-Carlo comparison in §30); accuracy of Laplace and MAP models is identical (0.926).
6. RBF SVM (encoded) is confirmed best by AUC, accuracy and F1; Naive Bayes on *raw* features (AUC 0.891) is better than on encoded (0.840).
